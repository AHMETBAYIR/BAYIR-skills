# From a lecture to a note that teaches

A slide deck is a speaking aid, not a textbook. Slides repeat, omit the "why",
and leave exercises unsolved. The note must let a student reconstruct the
lecture without reopening the slides.

## 1. Inspect the source before writing

If the user gives slides, a PDF, a transcript, or photos:

1. Extract the text first (`pdftotext -layout`, or the course's transcript).
2. Render pages that contain diagrams, equations, tables, or handwriting
   (`pdftoppm -png -r 150`) and read those images. Sparse text is not evidence
   that the page is empty.
3. Build a **coverage map** in the working file:

```
Central question:
Prerequisites:
Main concepts (in lecture order):
Definitions / laws:
Derivations:
Worked examples:
Figures / tables:
Exercises left open:
Administrative content (omit unless relevant):
```

Read the map back against the source. Every substantive concept gets a place in
the note; administrative announcements do not, unless they affect the exam.

## 2. Teach, do not inventory

Organise around a connected explanation, not a list of slide titles:

1. **What this is about** — the problem, why it matters, what the student will
   be able to do.
2. **Background and notation** — the minimum needed to follow; a small symbol
   table is often better than a paragraph.
3. **Main idea** — motivation → mechanism → consequence.
4. **Worked example** — setup, solution, check.
5. **Interpretation** — assumptions, limits, comparison, common confusion.
6. **Check your understanding** — 2–5 questions with short answers.

Use the lecturer's terminology and notation. Explain a term before relying on
it. When you add an explanation the slides do not contain, say so once ("bu
notta ek olarak..."). Never present an invented derivation as the lecturer's
own.

## 3. Preserve fidelity and flag errors

- Definitions, laws, and theorem statements should follow the source wording;
  explain around them in your own words.
- If a slide has an obvious typo, wrong sign, or missing case, put the correction
  in a `Dikkat` callout: "Slaytta ... yazıyor; doğrusu ...". Do not silently
  change the statement.
- If a value is unreadable, write "kaynakta okunamıyor" and do not guess.
- Keep the lecture's level. A first-year note does not need measure theory; a
  graduate note does not need a motivational metaphor for every term.

## 4. Formulas and reasoning

For every important formula, check:

- every symbol is defined before it appears;
- units are consistent and written upright;
- the sign convention matches the circuit/coordinate diagram;
- the assumptions are stated (ideal op-amp, small-signal, steady state, ...);
- the limiting cases behave (`t→0`, `t→∞`, `R→0`, `ω→0`, ...);
- the derivation's steps each have a reason, not just an equals sign.

For a proof or derivation, show the logical bridge. If the lecture only sketched
it, say "ispatın ana fikri" and stop; do not invent the missing details.

## 5. Figures earn their place

One figure per idea at most. A figure should answer a question the prose cannot
answer efficiently:

- a circuit for the topology;
- a waveform for the time behaviour;
- a block diagram for the signal path;
- a table for a comparison;
- a number line or tree for an ordering.

Every figure gets:

- a caption that states **what it shows** and **what to notice**;
- axes, units, labels, and component values;
- a check that labels do not overlap and the image is readable at 360 px and A4;
- a `Şekil N.` label generated by the template, not typed by hand.

Decorative images, generic stock diagrams, and AI-generated illustrations do
not belong in a study note.

## 6. Exercises and review

End with 2–5 exercises that test the lecture's actual skills, not trivia:

- one routine application;
- one that changes an assumption or asks "what if";
- one that connects two concepts;
- optionally one exam-style longer problem.

Put the solution under `<details class="solution">`; the print stylesheet opens
it automatically. The solution shows the method and names the rule at each step,
not only the final number.

## 7. Before delivery

- [ ] Coverage map: every substantive slide concept is present.
- [ ] Definitions and laws match the source; corrections are flagged.
- [ ] Every symbol is defined; units and signs are consistent.
- [ ] Every figure has a teaching caption and is checked at phone and A4 width.
- [ ] Every exercise has a solution and the solution has a check.
- [ ] No raw `$...$` remains in the rendered page.
- [ ] The TOC links work; the print preview does not split a callout or figure.
- [ ] The note says what was added beyond the source, in one short line.

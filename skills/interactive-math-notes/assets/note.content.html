Secant to tangent

Riemann sums and order

Left and right sums are first order; midpoint and trapezoid are second order; Simpson is fourth order for smooth functions. The table is more instructive than a single estimate: double n and read the error ratio.

Slope fields and one-step methods

A first-order equation y' = f(x,y) gives a direction at every point. Euler follows the direction for a whole step; Heun samples a second slope; RK4 combines four. The same step size makes the comparison fair.

Surfaces, contours, tangent planes

A function of two variables has three representations on one page: the formula, the surface, and the contour map. The gradient lives in the xy-plane and is perpendicular to the level curve. The tangent plane is the linearisation.

Root finding and convergence

Bisection is guaranteed but linear. Newton is quadratic near a simple root but can fail. Secant is superlinear and derivative-free. The table exposes the difference between a small step and a small residual.

Interpolation and the Runge phenomenon

A polynomial of degree n − 1 fits n points exactly, but exact interpolation is not the same as a good approximation. Equidistant nodes can oscillate; Chebyshev nodes cluster at the ends and control the maximum error.

Linear maps and eigenvectors

A matrix is a map, not just a table of numbers. The unit square becomes a parallelogram with area |det A|; the eigen-directions are the lines that do not rotate.

What to copy into a real note

Take-away

  • One lab should answer one question; two or three labs are usually enough for a full note.
  • Put the prediction before the controls and the explanation in the status line.
  • Keep the prose self-sufficient: a printed copy must still teach the definition, the example, and the mistake.
  • Use MatLivre or SymPy to check every number that appears in the readout or the prose.

Exercise 1

Use the slope lab with h = 0.5 and then h = 0.25. Compute the ratio of the Euler errors at x = 2. Does it look first order?

Solution

Halving h should roughly halve the Euler error if the method is first order. If the ratio is near 2, the experiment is consistent with O(h); if not, check the number of steps and the exact value.

Exercise 2

In the surface lab, move the point to (1, 0) and then to (0, 1). Explain why the gradient is parallel to the x-axis in the first case and to the y-axis in the second.

Solution

For f = x² + y², ∇f = (2x, 2y). At (1,0) it is (2,0); at (0,1) it is (0,2). The level curves are circles, so the radial gradient is perpendicular to the tangent circle at each point.
